\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note explains the v3 controller/estimator simulation campaign end to end: the
question it answers (\S1), the simulated plant, sensors and estimator (\S2), the
feasibility screening that defines which trajectories are fair to ask for (\S3), the
scalar objective every run minimizes (\S4), the five-stage protocol (\S5), the headline
findings (\S6), and the reading rules that keep the numbers honest (\S7). It is the
narrative companion to \texttt{hybrid\_ctrl\_v2/RESULTS\_v3.md}, which remains the
document of record for the numbers themselves.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}
The project needs a defensible answer to: \emph{which control law should the
omnidirectional platform fly, and how much of its performance survives real state
estimation?} Three candidate laws are compared, chosen so the comparison is a two-step
ablation:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l p{8.6cm} p{4.9cm} @{}}
\toprule
Controller & Structure & What it isolates\\
\midrule
\textbf{PID-FB} & cascaded IMC-reparameterized PID, 3 tuned $\lambda_{inner}$ per axis,
                  rest derived & industrial baseline\\
\textbf{PID-CT} & same cascade $+$ computed-torque feedforward (matched to ASMC's
                  equivalent control) & FB$\to$CT: value of the \emph{model}\\
\textbf{ASMC v2} & adaptive-gain supertwisting SMC, friction-circle gain ceiling
                  recomputed per tick & CT$\to$ASMC: value of \emph{adaptation}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All three are tuned by the same black-box optimizer against the same scalar objective on
the same trajectory tiers, then evaluated through the same frozen state estimator.
Anything that makes one controller's objective differ from another's is treated as a bug,
not a detail. MPC was part of the original plan and is deferred out of this iteration
(diverges on four velocity-demanding trajectories; its \texttt{use\_ltv=false}-is-better
anomaly remains unresolved).

\section{The system under test}
\paragraph{Plant.} The 30-state voltage-input plant (\texttt{hybrid\_ctrl/plant.jl}):
slip--spin--LuGre contact physics (per-wheel bristle states, passive roller spin) driven
through a DC-motor model with back-EMF,
\begin{equation}
\tau = G\eta\,(K_t i - \tau_f),\qquad i=(V-K_bG\omega)/R_a,\qquad |V|\le24\ \text{V},
\end{equation}
with a 200 V/s slew clamp in the mixer. Actuator authority falls linearly with wheel
speed and vanishes at the no-load speed $V_{max}/(K_bG)=27.73$ rad/s --- the hard
feasibility wall of \S3. (Derived in
\texttt{docs/Mecanum\_Analytical\_Limits\_AxisVel\_AccelEnvelope\_BackEMF.tex}.)

\paragraph{Sensors (\texttt{:realistic} suite).} IMU and wheel encoders at 1000 Hz;
optical flow at 100 Hz with 2\% dropout; 100 Hz pose fixes with per-run biases and 0.5\%
outliers --- industrial white noise plus deliberately uncalibrated consumer-grade biases.
Neither the estimator nor (in the deployable configuration) the controllers ever see the
true state.

\paragraph{Estimator.} \texttt{ESKFEstimatorV3}: a 13-state error-state Kalman filter
with a yaw-acceleration random-walk state (a type-2 tracker, added to remove a
structural yaw-rate lag). It is tuned once and \textbf{frozen} before any controller
comparison --- the \emph{estimator-first methodology} --- so a control-law difference can
never be an observer difference in disguise.

\paragraph{Three feedback regimes --- not two.} This distinction runs through the whole
campaign; getting it wrong inverts conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{clean oracle} --- perfect state feedback; an upper bound.
\item \textbf{noisy oracle} --- true state \emph{plus raw, unfiltered sensor noise}
      written into the controller's state estimate. There is \emph{no estimator} in this
      path; it is a pessimistic no-filtering bound, not a deployment model.
\item \textbf{ESKF} --- the deployable path: \texttt{:realistic} sensors through the
      frozen filter.
\end{enumerate}
The ESKF beats the noisy oracle \emph{by construction} (a tuned filter beats raw noise),
so ``ESKF vs noisy oracle'' is \textbf{not} the cost of state estimation --- that cost is
ESKF vs \emph{clean} oracle.

\section{Feasibility first: the v3 trajectory tiers}
The predecessor tier (\texttt{train12}) contained trajectories the hardware cannot fly,
and the tuner was spending most of its budget on them: \texttt{spiral\_orbit\_stress}
alone carried 72--81\% of the training tracking term while sitting at 85.4\% of the
motor's no-load speed. The v3 tiers (\texttt{train14\_v3}, \texttt{test\_v3}) screen
every candidate reference over its own full duration against two walls:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Motor / back-EMF (hard):} max per-wheel speed $(V_x\pm V_y\pm
(l{+}h)\dot\psi)/R$ against the 27.73 rad/s no-load speed; past $\sim$85\% of no-load no
gain law can arrest error growth. v3 targets $\le70\%$ for margin.
\item \textbf{Friction circle (soft):} per-wheel contact utilisation. Brief excursions
are momentary slip the LuGre plant absorbs --- measured to be harmless --- so this wall
informs but does not veto.
\end{enumerate}
The point of the rebuild: a controller comparison is only meaningful where the hardware
can actually go.

\section{The objective --- one scalar, three terms}
\begin{equation}
\text{score} \;=\; \text{tracking}_{v3} \;+\; 0.05\,(\text{ce}/V_{MAX}) \;+\;
0.36\,(\text{chatter}/\text{CHATTER}_{REF}).
\end{equation}
\textbf{tracking$_{v3}$}: six tolerance-normalised terms (position and heading $\times$
terminal / peak / typical), divided by a per-trajectory extent scaler $k_{traj}$. The v2
metric scored four samples out of 12k--60k per run (terminal and argmax only) and was
blind to mid-path oscillation; the time-normalised integral (``typical'') term is the
fix, with tolerances calibrated from measured achieved ratios. A term of 1.0 means
``exactly at target''. \textbf{ce}: mean summed wheel-voltage magnitude (energy proxy).
\textbf{chatter}: mean per-tick total variation of the wheel-voltage command against
$\text{CHATTER}_{REF}=0.8$ V/ms, the mixer's own slew clamp. This term exists because an
entire arc of v2 tuning kept railing on chatter the objective could not price (chatter
had been normalised by $V_{MAX}$ --- a voltage dividing a slew rate, $\sim$30--100$\times$
too weak). $\lambda_{chatter}=0.36$ is \emph{balance-preserving, not re-derived}: it
reproduces the accepted v2 tracking share (29.9\%) under the rescaled v3 metric, and the
objective code refuses $\lambda_{chatter}>1.44$ under \texttt{--metric v3} so a
carried-over v2 value cannot pass silently.

\paragraph{The estimator has its own objective.} A \emph{replay} score (estimator vs
ground truth on logged trajectories, no controller in the loop), same
tolerance-normalised shape, with velocity error \emph{during slip} priced twice
(\texttt{lam\_slip}=1.0): the point of an estimator on this platform is that its model
mismatch \emph{is} slip. It converged to $\sim$71\% a velocity-estimation objective,
matching what the controllers' inner loops consume.

\section{The protocol --- five stages, in order}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Estimator tuning (first, then frozen).} 5 seeds over a 7-dim log-scaled
      gain space, replay objective, \texttt{:realistic} sensors. All seeds converge to
      the same estimator within 0.23\% on a common noise realisation; seed 4 adopted and
      frozen for the whole campaign --- including tiers it was not tuned on.
\item \textbf{Feasibility screening} of trajectory tiers (\S3).
\item \textbf{Clean controller tuning} --- 5 seeds each, cold start, oracle-clean
      feedback, \texttt{train14\_v3}, convergence by plateau detection (the v1 tuner was
      caught terminating mid-descent, which invalidated its scatter claims).
\item \textbf{Noisy controller tuning} --- 4 seeds each, warm-started from the same run's
      clean optimum, feedback = noisy oracle. Run for all three controllers or none, so
      a noisy-tuned ASMC is never quoted against clean-tuned PIDs.
\item \textbf{Evaluation under three feedback regimes} --- the six converged configs
      (3 controllers $\times$ clean/noisy-tuned) on fresh sensor seeds 101--105:
      oracle-clean, oracle-noisy, and through the frozen ESKF (420 runs: $6\times14$
      trajectories $\times$ 5 seeds, zero non-converged). The held-out
      \texttt{test\_v3} leg (generalisation of the final estimator$+$controller stack)
      completes the campaign: 288 oracle sims $+$ 240 ESKF runs --- \S6 and
      \texttt{RESULTS\_v3.md} \S9.
\end{enumerate}
Throughout: one OS process per seed (the pipeline has global mutable state), BLAS pinned
to one thread for determinism, \texttt{stop\_reason} recorded per run, and outputs under
\texttt{runs\_*/} never hand-edited.

\section{Headline results}
Full tables and caveats in \texttt{RESULTS\_v3.md}; the structural findings:

\paragraph{PID-CT wins every regime.} Clean oracle 0.14340 vs ASMC 0.15318 (no seed
overlap; ordering PID-CT $<$ PID-FB $<$ ASMC); noisy oracle; and frozen-ESKF closed loop
0.34127, paired 69/70 and 70/70 against ASMC and PID-FB.

\paragraph{Noisy tuning does not transfer to deployment.} It helps against the noisy
oracle it was tuned on (ASMC 5/5, CT 5/5 paired) but the advantage vanishes through the
real ESKF, and it \emph{damaged} ASMC's heading $\sim$2$\times$ everywhere. Deploy
clean-tuned configs.

\paragraph{Real state estimation costs $+0.20$ of score, uniformly.} Against clean
oracle, essentially identical across all six configs (42.9--45.1\% of the
clean$\to$unfiltered gap recovered): the estimator's contribution is
controller-independent --- exactly what makes a frozen observer a fair basis for
comparison.

\paragraph{The error budget is a three-stage story.} Sensor injection (identical across
configs by construction) $\to$ ESKF survival (74--79\% of injected velocity error
removed; pose removal flat at $\sim$67.6\% on every trajectory) $\to$ controller
tracking, where essentially \emph{all} inter-controller spread lives. The estimator is
common mode; only the tracking stage discriminates control laws.

\paragraph{CT's one weakness is heading under sustained yaw.} Best heading on 9 of 14
trajectories ($\sim$1.1 mrad), collapsing to 29.8/37.6 mrad on exactly the two
sustained-yaw stress trajectories --- the same two where its yaw integral clamp fires on
17--20\% of ticks (0.0\% elsewhere). Three independent measurements agree; whether the
clamp binding \emph{harms} (vs merely binds) is not yet established.

\paragraph{Much of the tier is at the estimator's noise floor.} On 7 of 14 trajectories,
tracking error ($\sim$2--3 mm) sits at or below the estimator's own pose accuracy
($\sim$3.2 mm): further controller improvement there is unmeasurable with this sensor
suite, so the comparison is decided by the handful of trajectories where tracking rises
clear of the floor.

\paragraph{Heading is the ESKF's best channel; yaw rate is its worst.} Heading sees
6.9$\times$ error removal once an 11 ms startup transient on
non-zero-initial-heading references is accounted for; yaw rate sees only $\sim$52\%
removal --- the gyro is its only source, with no absolute fix to lean on.

\paragraph{Generalisation (\texttt{test\_v3}).} The held-out tier answers whether any of
the above transfers to trajectories the tuning never saw. Three cautions and one
headline:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Anchor caveat.} \texttt{coupled\_vomega\_anchor} is combo-identical to the
      training tier's \texttt{coupled\_vomega\_stress} --- a deliberate cross-tier
      consistency check, which passes bit-exactly ($\pm0.000000$) --- and must be
      excluded from every generalisation claim. It is the worst-tracked entry in either
      tier, so leaving it in would dominate any mean. The tier therefore yields
      \textbf{7 genuinely held-out trajectories}, 3 on profiles never seen in tuning.
\item \textbf{PID-CT generalises as the winner, unambiguously.} Clean-tuned PID-CT beats
      ASMC and PID-FB \textbf{35/35 paired} each on the held-out set and is first on
      every individual held-out trajectory --- the campaign's most robust result.
\item \textbf{The ASMC-vs-PID-FB ordering does not generalise.} Their training-tier gap
      was carried by a single trajectory (\texttt{coupled\_vomega\_stress}, 66\% of
      ASMC's gap); excluding it, they are statistically indistinguishable (23/35 and
      8/35). The defensible claim: \textbf{ASMC and PID-FB are equivalent, and PID-CT
      beats both.}
\item \textbf{The estimator is tier-invariant.} Every error-budget channel lands within
      $\sim$3 percentage points of its training value, and on all 7 held-out
      trajectories every controller tracks to 1.6--4.2 mm / 1.0--2.5 mrad. The heading
      collapses occur only on the anchor --- a \emph{training} trajectory --- consistent
      with CT's yaw-clamp weakness being a sustained-yaw property, not a generalisation
      failure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Reading rules}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Never mix tuning-stage scores with eval scores} --- different noise
      realisations, overlapping ranges.
\item \textbf{Pair, don't average, across realisations} --- realisation variance
      ($\sim$6--7\% eval, $\sim$16\% tuning) swamps controller differences
      ($\sim$2--4\%). Every ordering claim is a paired 5/5 or 4/4, never a difference of
      marginal means.
\item \textbf{Best-seed selection under noise is confounded} --- seed 4 is both the best
      seed and the easiest realisation for all three controllers; applied identically,
      so cross-controller comparison holds.
\item \textbf{Absolute values, never \% degradation} --- a better clean tracker reads a
      worse \% for the same absolute result.
\item \textbf{Three feedback regimes, not interchangeable} --- \S2.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Deliberately not done (yet)}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{MPC} --- deferred (\S1).
\item \textbf{\texttt{test\_v3} generalisation} --- \textbf{done} (\S6): PID-CT
      generalises 35/35; ASMC/PID-FB tie. Caveat: the tier yields 7 held-out
      trajectories, not 8 (anchor is combo-identical to a training entry).
\item \textbf{CT yaw integral budget sensitivity} --- the clamp is measured to
      \emph{bind}; that it \emph{harms} is unmeasured.
\item \textbf{ESKF yaw-rate re-weighting} --- interacts with the CT heading finding; the
      obvious next experiment.
\item \textbf{$\hat\psi$ initialisation from the first pose fix} --- removes the 11 ms
      startup transient that makes heading statistics incomparable across
      mixed-initial-heading tiers.
\end{itemize}

\section{Follow-up ablation: the friction-circle safety margin ($s\,{=}\,1.0$)}
\label{sec:s100}

Every result so far carries a $0.9$ factor of safety on the \emph{available} friction
circle (the same circle the feasibility envelope of
\texttt{Mecanum\_Analytical\_Limits\_AxisVel\_AccelEnvelope\_BackEMF.tex} derives;
\S\ref{app:plant}). The supervisor's question: is that margin hiding
performance? The factor enters exactly two controller gates --- ASMC's
\texttt{kmax\_schedule} gain ceiling and PID-CT's \texttt{vcmd\_limits} command gate
$\gamma$ (the $V_{ff}$ ceiling) --- so the ablation re-runs the \emph{entire} v3 chain
for ASMC and PID-CT with \texttt{safety\_margin}~$=1.0$, single-variable, same seeds
and optimizer budgets. PID-FB is out of scope (no feedforward). Verified gate deltas:
$K_{max}$ free-circle ceiling $\times 1.1111$ ($=1/0.9$ exactly), loaded circle
$\times 1.1452$ (nonlinear), $\gamma$ $0.4388\to0.4876$; the default path is
byte-identical to the archived campaign, outputs auto-suffixed \texttt{\_s100}, and the
margin is recorded in each config and read back at eval time, so gates can never be
mixed across campaigns. Full tables: \texttt{hybrid\_ctrl\_v2/RESULTS\_v3\_s100.md}.

\paragraph{Clean tuning: a real, consistent gain.} All ten $s{=}1.0$ seeds beat their
family's archived best --- ASMC $0.15197$ vs $0.15318$ ($-0.8\%$), PID-CT $0.14175$ vs
$0.14340$ ($-1.2\%$). This is the regime where the gates bind.

\paragraph{But it does not survive noise.} The noisy-tuning objective is flat to
slightly worse (paired means $+0.0039$ ASMC, $+0.0011$ PID-CT against a realisation
std of $\approx 0.037$); oracle noisy-means move $\le 0.003$ on \texttt{train14\_v3}.
Through the frozen ESKF, only one config moves materially: ASMC clean-tuned improves on
both tiers ($-0.0034$ / $-0.0061$) with slightly lower chatter everywhere; the rest are
unchanged. The ordering \textbf{PID-CT $<$ ASMC is untouched at every stage and tier},
and the noisy-tuning findings replicate (helps ASMC $>$ PID-CT, 5/5 seeds, smaller at
the relaxed margin).

\paragraph{The tuner never rails the new ceilings.} ASMC's clean optimum moves to
\emph{lower} surface slopes ($\lambda_x^{max}$ $5.68\to3.48$); PID-CT's
$\lambda_{inner,x}$ doubles ($0.098\to0.256$). The margin was shaping the achievable
gain landscape, not truncating an optimum sitting against it.

\paragraph{Significance.} Paired on (trajectory $\times$ noise seed), run-level
($n=70/40$) and traj-level (seeds averaged, $n=14/8$), paired $t$ + Wilcoxon
(\texttt{stats\_significance\_s100.py}):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Controller gap:} PID-CT $-$ ASMC is significant at both margins, both
      tiers, both tuning regimes --- worst case $p=9.7\!\times\!10^{-4}$, Cohen's
      $d_z$ $0.56$--$0.87$. The ordering is a property of the platform, not of the
      margin.
\item \textbf{Margin effect:} of eight tests, only \textbf{ASMC clean-tuned} is
      significant --- worst $p=0.015$, 21/22 trajectories better, \texttt{test\_v3}
      $d_z=-1.46$, Holm-surviving. PID-CT ct's \texttt{test\_v3} move ($p_t=0.059$) is
      a trend, not a finding.
\end{itemize}
Scope: inference covers the sensor-noise distribution and this fixed trajectory set
(design points, not random draws); and even the significant effect is $\sim$1\% of
score --- real, consistent, small.

\paragraph{Verdict.} The $0.9$ factor of safety costs $\sim$1\% of clean-regime
tracking-objective headroom and buys nothing statistically robust under noise beyond
one small ASMC-ct improvement. Keeping it or not is now a quantified design call.

\subsection{Plant equations (what the simulator integrates)}
\label{app:plant-eqs}

Per-contact slip velocities (representative roller phase $\tilde\varphi_i\to0$;
roller-spin rate $\dot\gamma_i$, wheel rate $\omega_i$):
\begin{align}
V_{px,i}&=V_x-\dot\psi\,p_{y,i}-\omega_i R+\dot\gamma_i\sin\delta_i(R_a-R),\\
V_{py,i}&=V_y+\dot\psi\,p_{x,i}+\dot\gamma_i\cos\delta_i(R-R_a),
\qquad \omega_{z,i}=\dot\psi .
\end{align}

Steady LuGre/Stribeck contact force (bounded, aligned with slip):
\begin{equation}
\vec F_i=-N_i\,g_t(\|V_p\|)\,\frac{\vec V_{p,i}}{\|V_p\|},\qquad
g_t=\mu_c\Big[1+(s-1)e^{-(\|V_p\|/v_s)^2}\Big],
\end{equation}
with per-contact bristle states integrated in the full plant (30 states:
3 body velocities, $\psi$, 4 wheel angles, 4 wheel rates, 4 roller rates,
2 world positions, 12 bristle states).

Wheel (actuated) and roller (passive) dynamics:
\begin{align}
J_w\,\dot\omega_i &= M_i - p_1\omega_i - F_{x,i}R,\\
J_r\,\ddot\gamma_i &= -p_2\dot\gamma_i
 - F_{x,i}\sin\delta_i\,(R-R_a\cos\tilde\varphi_i)
 - F_{y,i}\cos\delta_i\,(R_a-R\cos\tilde\varphi_i)
 - M_{z,i}\sin\tilde\varphi_i\cos\delta_i .
\end{align}

Platform rows (COM offset retained; $M^{-1}$ applied to the RHS):
\begin{align}
\tilde m\,\dot V_x - ma_Y\ddot\psi &= \textstyle\sum_i F_{x,i} + m_s\dot\psi V_y + m a_X\dot\psi^2,\\
\tilde m\,\dot V_y + ma_X\ddot\psi &= \textstyle\sum_i F_{y,i} - m_s\dot\psi V_x + m a_Y\dot\psi^2,\\
-ma_Y\dot V_x + ma_X\dot V_y + I_s\ddot\psi &= \textstyle\sum_i\big(p_{x,i}F_{y,i}-(p_{y,i})F_{x,i}+M_{z,i}\big)
 - m\dot\psi(a_XV_x+a_YV_y).
\end{align}

Actuator (quasi-static DC motor, evaluated every RHS call):
\begin{equation}
i=\frac{V_{cmd}-K_bG\,\omega_i}{R_a^{(m)}},\qquad
M_i=G\eta\,(K_t i-\tau_f\,\mathrm{sign}\,\omega_i),
\qquad |V_{cmd}|\le V_{max},\ \ |\dot V_{cmd}|\le dV_{max}.
\label{eq:motormap-app}
\end{equation}
The torque--speed line and its landmarks:
\begin{equation}
\tau_{avail}(\omega)=\underbrace{G\eta\Big(\frac{K_tV_{max}}{R_a^{(m)}}-\tau_f\Big)}_{9.12\ \mathrm{N\,m}}
-\underbrace{\frac{G^2\eta K_tK_b}{R_a^{(m)}}}_{0.337\ \mathrm{N\,m\,s/rad}}|\omega|,
\qquad \omega_{nl}=\frac{V_{max}}{K_bG}=27.73\ \mathrm{rad/s}.
\label{eq:tauavail-app}
\end{equation}

No-slip wheel-speed map (used by feasibility screening and the estimator's wheel
Jacobian):
\begin{equation}
\omega_i=\frac{1}{R}\Big[V_x+\tan\delta_i\,V_y+\big(\rho_{Xi}\tan\delta_i-\rho_{Yi}\big)\dot\psi\Big]
=\frac{V_x\pm V_y\pm(l+h)\dot\psi}{R}.
\label{eq:wheelmap-app}
\end{equation}

Equivalent (augmented) 3-DOF model the controllers are designed on:
\begin{equation}
M_{aug}\dot{\vec v}+C(\dot\psi)\vec v+D_{eq}\vec v+\vec f_c(\vec v)=\vec F_p,\qquad
\vec v=(V_x,V_y,\dot\psi),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
M_{aug}=\begin{bmatrix}\tilde m&0&-ma_Y\\0&\tilde m&ma_X\\-ma_Y&ma_X&I_\psi\end{bmatrix},\quad
\tilde m=m_s+\frac{4J_w}{R^2},\quad I_\psi=I_s+\frac{4(l+h)^2}{R^2}J_w,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
D_{eq}=\mathrm{diag}\Big(\tfrac{4p_1}{R^2},\
\tfrac{4p_1}{R^2}+\tfrac{8p_2}{(R-R_a)^2},\
\tfrac{4p_1(l+h)^2}{R^2}+\tfrac{8p_2h^2}{(R-R_a)^2}\Big)\approx\mathrm{diag}(176,396,38).
\end{equation}

\section{The frozen estimator (ESKF v3) --- complete equations}
\label{app:eskf}

\textbf{State} (error-state KF, \texttt{ESKFEstimatorV3},
\texttt{hybrid\_ctrl\_v2/estimators\_v3.jl}; heading carried as $(c,s)=(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)$,
re-normalised each tick):
\begin{equation}
x = [\,V_x,\ V_y,\ \dot\psi,\ c,\ s,\ X,\ Y,\ b_x,\ b_y,\ s_x,\ s_y,\ b_g,\ \alpha\,]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{13},
\end{equation}
with accelerometer biases $(b_x,b_y)$, slip states $(s_x,s_y)$, gyro bias $b_g$, and the
13th state $\alpha$ = yaw acceleration (the v3 addition: a second integrator on the yaw
channel; a type-2 tracker follows a constant-acceleration ramp with zero steady-state
lag, which the type-1 v2 design structurally could not).

\subsection{Prediction (1 kHz, IMU acceleration as input)}
\begin{align}
V_x^+ &= V_x + dt\,(a_x + \dot\psi V_y - b_x), &
V_y^+ &= V_y + dt\,(a_y - \dot\psi V_x - b_y),\\
\dot\psi^+ &= \dot\psi + dt\,\alpha, &
\alpha^+ &= \alpha + w_\alpha,\\
c^+ &= c - dt\,\dot\psi\,s, &
s^+ &= s + dt\,\dot\psi\,c,\\
X^+ &= X + dt\,(V_x c - V_y s), &
Y^+ &= Y + dt\,(V_x s + V_y c),\\
s_x^+ &= (1-dt/\tau_{slip})\,s_x, &
s_y^+ &= (1-dt/\tau_{slip})\,s_y,
\end{align}
biases $(b_x,b_y,b_g)$ constant in prediction; $\tau_{slip}=0.06$ s,
$\sigma_{slip}=0.014$ m/s (measured from traces). Slip states are
\emph{mean-reverting} (Ornstein--Uhlenbeck) in both the state and the error Jacobian ---
v1's pure random walk has no stationary variance.

\subsection{Error dynamics and process noise}
$P^+ = FPF^\top + Q$, $F = I + dt\,A$, $A$'s nonzero entries read off the prediction
above, including the yaw double-integrator coupling $A_{3,13}=1$ and the slip decay
$A_{10,10}=A_{11,11}=-1/\tau_{slip}$. Process noise, all entries derived (none searched):
\begin{align}
Q_{11}=Q_{22} &= (dt\,\sigma_{acc})^2\,q_{scale}, &
q_{scale} &= 1+\alpha_{acc}\|a\|+\alpha_{yaw}|\dot\psi|,\\
Q_{33} &= (dt\,\sigma_{gyro})^2 q_{scale}, &
Q_{44}=Q_{55} &= \texttt{pose\_Qn\_heading},\\
Q_{66}=Q_{77} &= \texttt{pose\_Qn\_pos}, &
Q_{88}=Q_{99} &= 10^{-10}\ \text{(margin)},\\
Q_{12,12} &= \sigma_{rw}^2\,dt\ \text{(gyro random walk)}, &
Q_{13,13} &= q_\alpha\ \text{(tuned)} .
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
Q_{10,10}=Q_{11,11} = \frac{2\sigma_{slip}^2}{\tau_{slip}}\,dt\ \times
\begin{cases}g_{grip}&\text{gripping}\\1&\text{slipping}\end{cases}
\qquad (g_{grip}=\texttt{grip\_slip\_scale}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Wheel + gyro update (1 kHz)}
Encoder wheel speeds $\tilde\omega$ are least-squared through the O-config Jacobian
\eqref{eq:wheelmap-app} --- $H_\omega^\top H_\omega=\mathrm{diag}(4/R^2,\,4/R^2,\,4(l{+}h)^2/R^2)$,
so the body-velocity pseudo-measurement covariance is closed-form, exactly diagonal and
isotropic --- plus the gyro:
\begin{equation}
z=\begin{bmatrix}(H_\omega^{+}\tilde\omega)_{x}\\ (H_\omega^{+}\tilde\omega)_{y}\\ \tilde g_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
H=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&\cdots&s_x\leftarrow\!x_{10}&\cdots\\ 0&1&0&\cdots&s_y\leftarrow\!x_{11}&\cdots\\
0&0&1&\cdots&b_g\leftarrow\!x_{12}&\cdots\end{bmatrix}_{3\times13},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R=\mathrm{diag}(R_w,\,R_w,\,R_g),\qquad
R_w=\frac{\big(\sigma_\omega^2+(\mathit{sf}_\omega\,\bar\omega)^2\big)R^2}{4},\qquad
R_g=\sigma_{gyro}^2+(\mathit{sf}_{gyro}\,\dot\psi)^2 .
\end{equation}
While slipping ($R$ inflated $\times$\texttt{slip\_R\_inflate}; accel-bias gains zeroed),
and with an NIS gate at $\chi^2_{3,0.99}=11.34$ that on violation boosts the wheel rows
$\times$\texttt{r\_boost} and retries once. Slip detection is pinned, not tuned:
$\mathrm{slip} = \|(H_\omega^{+}\tilde\omega,\ \tilde g_z)-(V_x,V_y,\dot\psi)_{pred}\|$,
threshold $=3\sqrt{R_w}$. Joseph-form covariance update with symmetrisation throughout.

\subsection{Flow update (100 Hz) and pose fix (100 Hz)}
Flow (skipped entirely on dropout or loss-of-lock --- \emph{never} a zero substitution):
\begin{equation}
H_{flow}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-r_y&0&\cdots&0\\ 0&1&r_x&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}_{2\times13},
\qquad
R_{flow}=\big(\sigma_{flow0}+\sigma_{flow}\,|v|\big)^2+\frac{\texttt{quantum}^2}{12},
\end{equation}
lever arm $r=(0,0)$ in this campaign; the \emph{slip columns are zero} --- wheels measure
$V_x+s_x$, flow measures $V_x$, which is what breaks the velocity$\leftrightarrow$slip
degeneracy. NIS gate $\chi^2_{2,0.999}=13.82$; $\alpha$ informed only through covariance.

Pose fix (\texttt{:docking} tier): $z_4=(x,\,y,\,\cos\psi,\,\sin\psi)$, $H$ selects
$(X,Y,c,s)$, $R=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{pos}^2,\sigma_{pos}^2,\sigma_{\psi}^2,\sigma_{\psi}^2)$,
NIS gate $\chi^2_{4,0.999}=16.27$.

\textbf{Output to controllers:} $\hat x=[V_x,V_y,\dot\psi,\mathrm{atan2}(s,c),X,Y]$,
$\hat d=[s_x,s_y,0]$. Divergence guard: innovation NIS $>10^4$ on 10 consecutive ticks
rescales $P\times10$.

\subsection{Converged configuration (adopted seed 4)}
Tuned (\texttt{runs\_estimator\_v4\_mu0p5\_train12/seed4/best\_config.json}):
$\alpha_{acc}=0.0229$, $\alpha_{yaw}=0.0146$,
\texttt{grip\_slip\_scale}$=3.66\!\times\!10^{-4}$, \texttt{r\_boost}$=61.6$,
\texttt{slip\_R\_inflate}$=42.2$, \texttt{pose\_Qn\_heading}$=10^{-8}$,
$q_\alpha=0.0666$.
Pinned physical priors (``$P_0$ is a statement of knowledge''):
$P_0^{vel}=10^{-4}$, $P_0^{yaw}=10^{-3}$, $P_0^{head}=0.5$,
$P_0^{bias\,acc}=4\!\times\!10^{-4}$, $P_0^{bias\,gyro}=9\!\times\!10^{-6}$,
$P_0^{slip}=2\!\times\!10^{-4}$, $P_0^{pos}=0.25$, $P_0^{\alpha}=0.25$,

\section{The tuning pipelines, with algebra}
\label{app:tuning}

\subsection{Common optimizer protocol (all tunings)}
Two-phase staged search (\texttt{controller\_tuning/optimizer\_stage.jl}):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Global: dxNES} (BlackBoxOptim) on a cheap screen objective, per-seed
      Sobol-offset start, run in chunks with a \emph{plateau gate} (relative improvement
      below \texttt{rel\_tol} over a trailing window) and a hard eval cap
      (\texttt{--p1-cap} 250). Convergence is detected, never assumed --- the v1 tuner was
      caught terminating mid-descent.
\item \textbf{Local: BOBYQA} (NLopt \texttt{LN\_BOBYQA}) on the full objective inside a
      box centred on the phase-1 optimum (\texttt{--p2-cap} 60), with an explicit
      score-plateau check --- BOBYQA's own \texttt{xtol/ftol} criterion was measured to
      \emph{not} fire even when the score had plateaued.
\end{enumerate}
Controllers: 5 seeds clean (cold start), 4 seeds noisy (warm-start from the same run's
clean optimum). Estimator: 5 seeds, replay. One OS process per seed; BLAS single-threaded;
\texttt{stop\_reason} recorded per run.

\subsection{Controller objective (\texttt{stage\_objective.jl})}
\begin{equation}
\text{score} = \text{tracking}_{v3} + 0.05\,\frac{ce}{24} + 0.36\,\frac{chatter}{0.8},
\end{equation}
with the six-term tracking (errors in physical units, tolerances normalising each term;
$he=2|\sin(\Delta\psi/2)|$, $pe=\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2}$):
\begin{equation}
\text{tracking}_{v3}=\frac{1}{6\,k_{traj}}\Big(
\frac{pe_T}{0.01}+\frac{\max_t pe}{0.1}+\frac{\overline{pe}}{0.0249}
+\frac{he_T}{0.01}+\frac{\max_t he}{0.1}+\frac{\overline{he}}{0.0128}\Big),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
k_{traj}=\frac{\max\big(\max_t\|p_{ref}(t)-\bar p_{ref}\|,\ 0.1\big)}{0.1}\ \ge 1
\qquad\text{(path extent, rotation-invariant)},
\end{equation}
terminal / peak / typical per channel, $\overline{(\cdot)}$ the time-normalised mean
absolute. The 0.0249 / 0.0128 tolerances are \emph{measured} (achieved iae/max ratios
0.249 / 0.128 over 7 converged configs $\times$ 14 trajectories).
$ce=\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\sum_{i=1}^4|v_{cmd,i}|$ (voltage magnitude);
$chatter=\frac{1}{T}\sum_t\sum_i|\Delta v_{cmd,i}|$ per ms tick (slew rate), referenced
to the mixer's own clamp $4\,dV_{max}/f_{mix}=0.8$ V/ms.

\subsection{PID-FB / PID-CT --- IMC cascade, 3 tuned dims}
Per-axis inner plant $m_{eff}\dot v + d_{eff} v = W$ with
$m_{eff}=(R\tilde m,\,R\tilde m,\,I_\psi)\approx(2.25,2.25,5.89)$ and $d_{eff}$ the
$R$-scaled rows of $D_{eq}$. IMC pole cancellation:
\begin{equation}
K_p=\frac{m_{eff}}{\lambda},\qquad K_i=\frac{d_{eff}}{\lambda},\qquad K_d=0\ (\text{a
result, not a choice}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\frac{K_i}{K_p}=\frac{1}{\tau_{open}}\ \text{identically,}\qquad
\tau_{open}=\frac{m_{eff}}{d_{eff}}\approx(0.256,0.114,0.155)\ \mathrm{s}.
\end{equation}
Outer loop on the integrator plant $\dot p = v$: $K_{p,pos}=1/(N\lambda)$, $N=4$, giving
$\omega_n=1/(\lambda\sqrt N)$, $\zeta=\sqrt N/2=1$ (critically damped). Anti-windup is
\emph{derived}: $I_{max}=\texttt{wrench\_budget}/K_i$ with the budget the measured p95
integral demand, so $K_iI_{max}$ is invariant of $\lambda$. PID-CT adds the
computed-torque feedforward
\begin{equation}
W_{ff}=m_{eff}\,a_{cmd}+d_{eff}\,V_{cmd}+C(\dot\psi)\hat V ,
\end{equation}
structurally matched to ASMC's equivalent control. Tuned space
(\texttt{PID\_SPACE\_V2}, log): $\lambda_x\in[0.003,0.256]$,
$\lambda_y\in[0.003,0.114]$, $\lambda_\psi\in[0.003,0.155]$ --- upper bounds
\emph{are} $\tau_{open}$ (beyond which the loop is slower than open loop); the lower
bound is a numerical guard at $3\,dt$, the real floor being priced endogenously by the
chatter term ($K_p=m_{eff}/\lambda$ amplifies command noise as $\lambda\to0$).

\subsection{ASMC v2 --- adaptive supertwist, 4 tuned dims}
Surface per axis with a saturation-aware slope, $\lambda(e)=\min(\lambda_{max},\
v_{max}/|e|)$; smooth switching wrench $W = W_{eq} + K\,\tanh(s/\varepsilon)$ with the
boundary layer \emph{derived} from estimator noise,
$\varepsilon_i=\max(0.5\,\text{tol}_i,\ k\,\sigma_{s,i})$.
Gain adaptation (per axis) with the friction-circle ceiling recomputed per tick and the
demand-driven decay:
\begin{equation}
\dot K_i=\gamma_i\Big[s_i\tanh(s_i/\varepsilon_i)\,(1-z_i)-\rho_{auth}\,\varepsilon_i\,z_i\Big],
\qquad
z_i=\frac{K_i-K_{floor}}{K_{max,sched,i}-K_{floor}},
\end{equation}
so $K$ grows under sliding-mode activity, parks below the scheduled ceiling $K_{max,sched}$
(the wheel-3 friction budget, \texttt{kmax\_schedule}), and decays toward the floor at a
rate set by the demand scale $\rho_{auth}\varepsilon$; $\gamma$ is a pure time-scaling
(fixed at the derived per-axis values $170/380/280$ after a $64\times$ sweep showed it
score-inert). Tuned space (\texttt{ASMC\_SPACE\_V2}, log):
$\lambda_{x,max},\lambda_{y,max}\in[0.3,10]$, $\lambda_{\psi,max}\in[1,20]$,
$\rho_{auth}\in[0.762,27]$ (both ends physically anchored: $\tanh(1)$ and the
$\sigma$-leak excitation scale).

\subsection{Estimator tuning --- replay objective, 7 tuned dims}
The estimator is tuned \emph{open loop} (replay against ground truth on logged
trajectories; no controller in the loop), same tolerance-normalised shape:
\begin{equation}
\text{score}_{est}=\frac{vel_{rmse}}{10^{-3}}+\frac{rate_{rmse}}{10^{-2}}
+1.0\cdot\frac{inslip\_vel_{rmse}}{10^{-3}}
+\frac{pos_{rmse}}{10^{-2}}+\frac{head_{rmse}}{10^{-2}}
+0.05\cdot\text{smoothness},
\end{equation}
velocity units m/s, rates rad/s, positions m, headings rad;
$\text{smoothness}=\mathrm{mean}\big((d|\hat v|/dt)^2\big)$. The in-slip velocity error is
priced \emph{twice} (once in $vel_{rmse}$, once gated) --- the estimator's model mismatch
on this platform \emph{is} slip. Search space (\texttt{param\_space\_v4.jl}, all
log-scaled): $\alpha_{acc},\alpha_{yaw}\in[10^{-2},10^{2}]$,
\texttt{grip\_slip\_scale}$\in[10^{-5},1]$, \texttt{r\_boost}$\in[1,100]$,
\texttt{pose\_Qn\_heading}$\in[10^{-8},10^{-3}]$,
\texttt{slip\_R\_inflate}$\in[1,100]$, $q_\alpha\in[10^{-8},10^{-1}]$.
Conditions: \texttt{train12}, $\mu=0.5/\chi=0.005$, \texttt{:realistic} suite,
$f_{est}=1$ kHz, \texttt{fix\_tier=:docking}. All five seeds converge to the same
estimator within 0.23\% on a common noise realisation; seed 4 adopted and frozen.
